## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, in this order of importance for the current deliverable:

1. **The clinic owner, Dr. Mohamed Elbaz**, viewing a private homepage concept prepared by LeadSync and presented in a sales meeting (Google Meet). The job of the page is to show him the quality of website his clinic deserves.
2. **Prospective patients** in and around Sheikh Zayed (Giza, Egypt), mostly reading on phones, who want to understand what the clinic treats, trust it, and request an appointment. Arabic is their primary language. *(Inferred from location and market; not interviewed.)*

## Product Purpose

A homepage for a private physiotherapy clinic in Beverly Hills, Sheikh Zayed. It explains the services, sets expectations (assessment first, then a plan), and turns visits into appointment enquiries by phone or WhatsApp. Success for the concept: the owner commissions the full site. Success for the eventual site: more appointment enquiries from the right patients.

This is a **concept preview, not the clinic's official website**. It must say so visibly on every screen: "تصور مقترح من LeadSync — ليس الموقع الرسمي للعيادة".

## Positioning

Assessment before any treatment, and a plan explained in plain language. The clinic offers individual sessions in a calm private setting rather than a high-volume centre. *(Framing written by LeadSync; the clinic has not yet confirmed it.)*

- **Never fabricate** credentials, success rates, patient numbers, testimonials or outcome promises. Treatment recommendations require a professional assessment.

## Product Principles

1. **Truth over polish.** An honest "to be confirmed" beats an invented credential, number or review.
2. **Assessment first.** Every service and call to action leads to an assessment, never to a promised outcome.
3. **Make the next step obvious.** Contacting the clinic is never more than one tap away, and the page is clear that a request is not a confirmed appointment.
4. **Respect patient privacy.** Messages invite scheduling only, not medical details.
